## Why write down every sale
id: records-01
triggers: no_records, onboarding
topics: records, bookkeeping, sales

Your memory tells you the day was "good" or "bad". Records tell you *why*. They show which items make money, which customers owe you, and whether costs are creeping up.

Records also open doors. Lenders, suppliers who give stock on credit, and government programmes all ask: "Show me your numbers." A business with three months of daily records is far easier to fund.

Recording here takes seconds: "sold 3 bread 50", "spent 200 transport", or just send a voice note.

**Try this:** Record every sale and expense today, even small ones. Tonight, send *report*.

**Quiz:** Which business is easier for a lender to trust?
- A) One that says "business is good" but has no records
- B) Both are the same
- C) One with three months of daily sales and expenses *
**Why:** Lenders lend against evidence. Daily records show your real income and how steady it is.

## The daily cash count
id: records-02
triggers: no_records
topics: cash, count, close

At closing time, count the cash in the drawer and check your mobile money balance. Compare it with what your records say you should have (start cash + sales collected − expenses paid).

If the numbers don't match, something is missing: a sale not recorded, change given wrongly, or money taken without a note. Catching it the same day is easy. Catching it a month later is impossible.

**Try this:** Count your cash tonight and compare it with *report*. Note any difference.

**Quiz:** You started with 2,000, cash sales were 5,000 and you paid 1,000 for transport. How much cash should be in the drawer?
- A) 8,000
- B) 6,000 *
- C) 5,000
**Why:** 2,000 + 5,000 − 1,000 = 6,000. If you have less, find the missing money today.
