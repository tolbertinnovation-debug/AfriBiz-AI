# Module: Money basics
order: 1

## Keep business money and home money apart
id: money-01
triggers: owner_draws, onboarding
topics: separate, personal, salary, money

When money for the shop and money for the house sit in one pocket, you can't tell if the business is growing or shrinking. Many businesses that "always seem busy" fail because the owner keeps eating the stock money without noticing.

Use two places: one for the business (a separate wallet, a separate mobile money line, or M-PESA Pochi la Biashara / a MoMo merchant account) and one for home. When you need money for the house, *pay yourself* from the business and record it. Tell me: "took 500 for home".

**Try this:** Choose a fixed weekly amount to pay yourself, and keep everything else inside the business.

**Quiz:** You sold goods worth 1,000 today and took 300 home for food. What did you take home?
- A) A business expense
- B) An owner's drawing (your pay), not an expense *
- C) Nothing, because it was your own money
**Why:** Money you take for personal use is your pay (a "drawing"). It lowers business cash but it is not a cost of running the business, so record it separately.

## Pay yourself a salary
id: money-02
triggers: owner_draws
topics: salary, drawings

Taking "a little here, a little there" adds up faster than you think. A fixed salary for yourself makes your spending predictable and protects your stock money.

Start small: look at your profit for the last month (send *report month*), and pay yourself no more than half of it. Leave the rest in the business to buy more stock or to save for slow weeks.

**Try this:** Send *report month* and decide your weekly salary from the profit figure.

**Quiz:** Your monthly profit is 20,000. What is a safe salary to start with?
- A) 25,000, because you work hard
- B) 20,000, all of it
- C) About 10,000, leaving the rest to grow the business *
**Why:** Paying yourself more than the business earns eats your capital. Half of profit leaves room for stock, emergencies and growth.
