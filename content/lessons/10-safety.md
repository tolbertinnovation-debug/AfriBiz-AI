## The "sent by mistake" trick and fake SMS
id: safety-01
topics: fraud, scam, fake sms, mistake, safety

A common scam: a "customer" shows you an SMS on their phone, or sends you a message that looks like an M-PESA/MoMo confirmation, then takes the goods. Another: "I sent you money by mistake, please send it back."

Protect yourself:
• Only trust the balance on *your own* line (dial the official menu or open the official app) before you release goods.
• Real confirmations come from the official sender name (e.g. MPESA, MobileMoney), not a normal phone number.
• Never reverse a "mistake" yourself. Tell the person to contact the provider, who can reverse it properly.

If you forward a payment message to me, I check it against your last known balance and warn you if the numbers don't add up.

**Try this:** Practise checking your balance quickly with the official menu or app.

**Quiz:** A customer shows you a payment SMS on *their* phone. What do you do?
- A) Hand over the goods
- B) Ask them to forward it
- C) Check that the money is in *your* account first *
**Why:** Messages on someone else's phone can be fake. Only your own balance proves payment.

## Protect your PIN
id: safety-02
topics: pin, password, security

Your PIN is the key to your money. No real agent, provider, bank or AfriBiz will ever ask for it. Don't share it with workers; give them a separate line or a till with limited access instead.

Change your PIN if you think someone saw it, and lock your phone with a screen lock.

**Try this:** Set a screen lock on your phone today if you don't have one.

**Quiz:** Someone calls saying they are from your mobile money provider and asks for your PIN to "fix your account". What do you do?
- A) Give it, they are helping
- B) Hang up; providers never ask for your PIN *
- C) Give only half of it
**Why:** Anyone asking for your PIN is trying to steal from you.
