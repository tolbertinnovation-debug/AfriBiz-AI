# Module: Costing and pricing
order: 3

## Know what each item really costs you
id: pricing-01
triggers: low_margin, losing_money
topics: cost, costing, unit cost

The cost of an item is not only what you paid the supplier. Add transport to bring it, packaging, and any losses (spoilt, broken, eaten).

Example: a crate of 30 eggs costs 300, transport is 30, and 2 eggs usually break. Your real cost is 330 ÷ 28 ≈ 11.8 per egg, not 10. Selling at 12 leaves you almost nothing.

When you buy stock, tell me the cost ("bought 30 eggs for 330") and I will work out your profit on every sale.

**Try this:** Pick your three best-selling items and work out their full cost per unit, including transport.

**Quiz:** You buy 10 bags for 1,000 and pay 100 transport. What is your cost per bag?
- A) 100
- B) 90
- C) 110 *
**Why:** (1,000 + 100) ÷ 10 = 110 per bag. Forgetting transport makes you underprice.

## Setting a price that pays you
id: pricing-02
triggers: low_margin
topics: price, pricing, margin, markup

A simple method: *full cost + your margin*, then check the market.

1. Full cost per unit (from the last lesson).
2. Add a margin. Many small shops aim for 20-40% on top of cost, more for items people need urgently or that spoil.
3. Compare with nearby sellers. If you are much higher, offer something extra (closer, cleaner, delivery, credit for trusted customers). If you are much lower, you may be leaving money on the table.

Remember: *markup* is profit as a share of cost, *margin* is profit as a share of the selling price. Buying at 100 and selling at 125 is a 25% markup but a 20% margin.

**Try this:** Send *report week* and look at your gross margin. Is it above 20%?

**Quiz:** An item costs you 80. You want a 25% markup. What price do you set?
- A) 100 *
- B) 105
- C) 80.25
**Why:** 80 × 1.25 = 100. Your profit of 20 is 25% of the cost of 80.

## Discounts eat profit faster than you think
id: pricing-03
triggers: low_margin
topics: discount, bargain

If your margin is 20% and you give a 10% discount, you lose *half* your profit on that sale. You would need to sell twice as many items to earn the same.

Instead of cutting prices, offer value: a small free item on big orders, "buy 5 get 1" only on slow-moving stock, or a loyalty reward for regular customers.

**Try this:** Next time a customer bargains hard, offer a small extra instead of a price cut.

**Quiz:** Your margin is 20%. You give a 10% discount. How much of your profit on that sale is left?
- A) 90%
- B) About half *
- C) 80%
**Why:** On a 100 sale your profit was 20. After a 10 discount, profit is 10, which is half.
