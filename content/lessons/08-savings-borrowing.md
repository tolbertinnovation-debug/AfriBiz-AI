# Module: Saving and borrowing
order: 8

## An emergency fund keeps the doors open
id: savings-01
topics: savings, emergency, chama, susu, esusu, ajo

Illness, a theft, a flood or a bad month: emergencies happen. Without savings, owners often sell stock cheap or take expensive loans.

Aim for an emergency fund equal to at least one month of business expenses, kept somewhere you can't easily spend it: a savings account, a locked mobile wallet (M-Shwari lock savings, MoMo savings), or your chama/susu group.

**Try this:** Decide a daily amount to save, even a small one, and start today.

**Quiz:** Your monthly expenses are 15,000. What is a good first emergency fund target?
- A) 1,500
- B) 150,000
- C) 15,000 *
**Why:** One month of expenses lets you survive a bad month without selling stock at a loss.

## Borrow only for things that earn
id: savings-02
topics: loan, borrow, interest, debt
triggers: credit_low

A loan is good when it buys something that earns more than the loan costs: stock you will sell quickly, a fridge that lets you sell cold drinks. It is dangerous when it pays for consumption or covers losses.

Always ask: *what is the total I will pay back?* A loan of 10,000 with 10% "per month" for 3 months costs 3,000 in interest. Digital loans with small fees can be very expensive when you count them per year.

**Try this:** Before any loan, write: amount, total to repay, and exactly how the money will earn more than that.

**Quiz:** You borrow 10,000 to buy stock you sell for 13,000 within a month. Interest is 1,000. Was it worth it?
- A) Yes, you earned 3,000 and paid 1,000 *
- B) No, loans are always bad
- C) It depends on the weather
**Why:** The loan produced 3,000 gross profit for a cost of 1,000, which leaves 2,000 extra. That is a productive loan.
